Section A: Metric Spaces

1

For points $u = (u_1, u_2)$ and $v = (v_1, v_2)$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$, and for a real number $p \ge 1$, let $$d_p(u,v) = \bigl(|u_1 - v_1|^p + |u_2 - v_2|^p\bigr)^{1/p}, \qquad d_\infty(u,v) = \max\bigl\{|u_1 - v_1|,\ |u_2 - v_2|\bigr\}.$$

(a)

Show that $d_1$ and $d_\infty$ are metrics on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

[5]
(b)

Prove that, for all $u$ and $v$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$, $$d_\infty(u,v) \;\le\; d_2(u,v) \;\le\; d_1(u,v) \;\le\; 2\,d_\infty(u,v).$$ Deduce that a subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is open with respect to $d_1$ if and only if it is open with respect to $d_\infty$.

[7]
(c)

Show that $d_p(u,v) \to d_\infty(u,v)$ as $p \to \infty$, for every $u$ and $v$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

[4]
(d)

The formula for $d_p$ also makes sense when $0 < p < 1$. By considering the points $O$, $A$ and $B$ of Fig. 1, show that $d_{1/2}$ is not a metric on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Let $(X,d)$ be a non-empty complete metric space, and let $T \colon X \to X$ be a map for which there is a constant $c$ with $0 \le c < 1$ and $d(Tx, Ty) \le c\,d(x,y)$ for all $x, y \in X$.

(a)

Show that $T$ has at most one fixed point.

[2]
(b)

Let $x_0 \in X$ and let $x_{n+1} = T x_n$ for $n \ge 0$. Show that $$d(x_n, x_m) \;\le\; \frac{c^{\,n}}{1-c}\, d(x_0, x_1) \qquad \text{whenever } m > n \ge 0,$$ and deduce that $T$ has a fixed point.

[8]
(c)

A copy of this page, reduced in the ratio $\lambda : 1$ where $0 < \lambda < 1$, is laid flat upon the page and lies wholly within it, as in Fig. 2. Prove that exactly one point of the copy lies directly over the point of the page which it depicts.

[5]
(d)

Give an example of a non-empty complete metric space $(X,d)$ and a map $T \colon X \to X$ with $d(Tx,Ty) < d(x,y)$ whenever $x \ne y$, but with no fixed point. Justify your answer.

Section B: Series

3

For each integer $n \ge 1$ let $a_n = \dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$, so that $\sum a_n$ is the alternating harmonic series $1 - \tfrac12 + \tfrac13 - \tfrac14 + \cdots$.

(a)

Show that $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ converges, and that it does not converge absolutely.

[4]
(b)

Let $x$ be a real number. Prove that there is a bijection $\sigma \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ such that $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_{\sigma(n)} = x .$$

[9]
(c)

You are given that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n = \ln 2$. In the rearrangement $$1 + \tfrac13 - \tfrac12 + \tfrac15 + \tfrac17 - \tfrac14 + \tfrac19 + \tfrac1{11} - \tfrac16 + \cdots$$ two positive terms are followed by one negative term throughout. Find its sum.

[5]
(d)

State, with a reason, whether the result of part (b) still holds when $a_n$ is replaced by $(-1)^{n+1}/n^2$.

Section C: Measure

5

Throughout this question $m$ denotes Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$. Let $C_0 = [0,1]$ and, for $n \ge 1$, let $C_n$ be obtained from $C_{n-1}$ by deleting the open middle third of each of the $2^{n-1}$ intervals of which $C_{n-1}$ consists. The Cantor set is $C = \bigcap_{n \ge 0} C_n$.

(a)

Show that $C$ is compact and that $m(C) = 0$.

[5]
(b)

Show that $C$ contains no interval of positive length, and that $C$ is nevertheless uncountable.

[6]
(c)

Let $0 < a \le \tfrac13$, and let the set $F_a$ be constructed as $C$ was, except that at stage $n$ the open interval deleted from the middle of each of the $2^{n-1}$ intervals has length $a^n$. Show that $$m(F_a) = \frac{1 - 3a}{1 - 2a}.$$

[5]
(d)

Deduce that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a closed subset of $[0,1]$ which contains no interval of positive length and whose measure exceeds $1 - \varepsilon$.

Each integer $n \ge 1$ can be written in exactly one way as $n = 2^k + j$ with integers $k \ge 0$ and $0 \le j < 2^k$. Let $f_n \colon [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be the indicator function of the interval $\bigl[\,j/2^k,\ (j+1)/2^k\,\bigr]$, and let $m$ denote Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$.

(a)

Show that $\displaystyle\int f_n\,dm \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, and deduce that $f_n \to 0$ in measure.

[4]
(b)

Show that for every $x \in [0,1]$ the sequence $\bigl(f_n(x)\bigr)$ does not converge.

[5]
(c)

Find a subsequence of $(f_n)$ which converges to $0$ almost everywhere, and prove that it does so.

[5]
(d)

State the dominated convergence theorem. Explain why it cannot be applied to the sequence $(f_n)$, although its conclusion holds for this sequence.
